03 · why passports matter

EU product data. Bridge asset context.

The legal DPP and a bridge passport can connect, but they answer different questions.

EU

Construction Products Regulation · EU 2024/3110

Rules for construction products placed on the EU market

The revised CPR sets up a digital passport system for construction products. The passport is product-linked and is intended to carry information such as product performance and conformity, technical documentation, safety information and identifiers. It also aims to improve traceability and support reuse.

8 Jan 2026Main part of the revised CPR starts applying; product families transition at different times.
Q2 2027*Commission planning page lists a delegated act for construction product DPP requirements and service providers.
After the actArticle 80 sets an 18-month period for mandatory manufacturer DPP use through the system; detail depends on the applicable rules.

*Commission indicative planning date, checked 11 October 2026; dates and implementation can change.

NL

Where digiGO fits

Dutch digital collaboration for the built environment

digiGO helps align how construction and infrastructure partners share digital information. Its building-passport work shows a useful hierarchy for this idea: asset → element → product → component → material.

Bridge assetArch / elementProduct recordPart + material
A Dutch government agreement lists a work item for Rijkswaterstaat and the Central Government Real Estate Agency to develop a passport format for exchanging concrete data in infrastructure, housing and utility buildings. This is a relevant direction—not proof of an approved bridge passport standard.

So this demo uses the same practical idea: connect product-level information to the bridge asset and the reuse decision, while keeping each source and data gap visible.

Important distinction: EU law addresses construction products and product information. It does not, by itself, mean that every existing bridge must have one legal passport for its whole life. A bridge-level record is a proposed asset-management tool; whether a specific reused arch or product falls under a product obligation depends on the facts and applicable rules.
